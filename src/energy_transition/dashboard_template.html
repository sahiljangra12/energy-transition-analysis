Renewable energy and development, 2000–2021

How much of each country's energy comes from renewable sources (hydro, wind, solar, bioenergy), how that has changed since 2000, and how it relates to national income. Data for 215 countries and areas from the World Bank and the UN SDG database.

1. Where does electricity come from renewables?

Each country is shaded by the share of its electricity generated from renewable sources. Darker blue means more renewable. Drag the slider to see any year from 2000 to 2021, and hover over a country for its exact value.

Countries with large rivers and hydropower (Brazil, Norway, much of Central and Southern Africa) are darkest. Slide back to 2000 and compare: Germany, the UK and Spain get noticeably darker by 2021 as wind and solar grow.

2. Are richer countries more renewable?

Each dot is one country in 2021. Left to right is GDP per person (log scale, so each step is ten times richer). Bottom to top is the renewable share. Switch between the two measures to see why the answer depends on what you count.

What this shows

  • Renewable electricity doubled for the typical country, from 10% to 22% among the 187 countries that reported every year.
  • Most countries moved up: 74% of countries with over 1 million people raised their renewable electricity share between 2011 and 2021. Countries that started low gained the most.
  • Income is not the main driver of clean electricity. Geography (rivers for hydropower) and policy matter more. The steep income pattern for all energy mostly reflects poorer households cooking with wood and charcoal.

These are descriptive patterns, not causal effects. Regression models, limitations and the full method are in the research note.

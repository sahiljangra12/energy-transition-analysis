The 10 biggest risers and fallers

Change in the renewable share of electricity, in percentage points, among __N_CHANGE__ countries with over 1 million people. Click a bar to explore that country.

Rises came from new hydropower (Cambodia), from wind, solar and bioenergy replacing coal (Denmark, the UK), or from small grids where a few new plants shift the share (Liberia, Nicaragua). The biggest falls were in hydro-reliant countries that met growing demand with gas: Ghana's gas generation rose by about 11 TWh while its hydro stayed flat.

What this shows

These are descriptive patterns, not causal effects. Regression models, limitations and the full method are in the research note and the technical report (PDF).

  • Renewable electricity doubledfor the typical country, from 10% to 22% among the 187 countries that reported every year.
  • Most countries moved up74% of countries with over 1 million people raised their renewable electricity share between 2011 and 2021, and those that started low gained the most.
  • Income isn't the main driverGeography (rivers for hydropower) and policy matter more for clean electricity. The steep income pattern for all energy mostly reflects poorer households cooking with wood and charcoal.

Terms and data

Plain-language definitions of the measures used on this page, and the data behind every chart.

TWh
Terawatt-hours: one billion kilowatt-hours. A country of 10 million people in Europe uses roughly 60 TWh of electricity a year.
Renewable share of electricity
Electricity from hydro, wind, solar, bioenergy, geothermal and other renewables, as a percentage of all electricity generated.
Renewable share of all energy use
Renewables as a share of total final energy, which also covers heating, cooking and transport. In the poorest countries this is mostly wood and charcoal (traditional biomass), not modern clean energy.
Median, same countries
The middle value across the __N_BAL__ countries that report every year from 2000 to 2021, so changes reflect real shifts rather than new countries entering the data.
Population-weighted mean
The share for the average person rather than the average country; large countries such as China and India count for more.
Income quarter
Countries split into four equal groups by GDP per person in 2021, from poorest to richest.
